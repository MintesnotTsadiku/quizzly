# Bluffline — challenging-mechanic illustration draft

Review candidate generated using the built-in image tool. Common Ground draft 2 was the style reference. The live application and other artwork are unchanged.

Asset: `quizzly/public/images/games/bluffline/how-to-ethiopian-v1.png`.

Selection: Bluffline combines private authored bluffs, anonymous ballots excluding the voter's own submission, and scoring both for finding truth and attracting votes. These mechanics and the 250 / 500 / 1,000 point awards were checked against `quizzly/games/round_games/game.py`, in `candidates`, `submit_action`, and `reveal`.

The illustration follows one concrete answer through authorship, another player's ballot and the shared reveal. It depicts one device per player; it is an instructional diagram, not a screenshot of the current game UI. No library-wide rollout or separate task has been started.

## Exact generation prompt

Create a new HOW-TO-PLAY illustration for the game BLUFFLINE. Use the supplied Common Ground image ONLY as a visual style reference: warm cream paper, subtle grain, plum outlines and lettering, peach/lavender/muted-lime colors, expressive contemporary Ethiopian family members in normal everyday clothes. Do NOT copy its game instructions, phone requirements or no-points rule. Landscape 3:2, production-quality illustrated storyboard. The image must teach this more complex game using one concrete worked example. Ethiopian people with varied medium-brown to deep-brown skin and naturally curly/coily hair, everyday T-shirts/cardigans/trousers, comfortable modern middle-class living room. No traditional costumes or caricatures. Keep recurring characters' faces, clothes and hair consistent.

Title "BLUFFLINE". Subtitle "Make up a bluff. Find the truth."
Near the title a small phone icon and exact text "One phone per player".
Three clearly numbered spacious scenes, left to right, connected by arrows. Roughly half of each panel is human interaction; half is a large readable illustration of what they see on their phone or shared screen. Do not draw an app screenshot. Use diagram callouts integrated with the illustrations. Every depicted player has their own phone; answers stay private until voting, and author names are hidden until the reveal.

The illustrative prompt is "What is a group of flamingos called?" with two small flamingo drawings. The true answer in this example is Flamboyance; one player's invented answer is Festival; another invented answer is Parade. All answer strings must retain the same spelling throughout.

Scene 1 heading "1  Invent a bluff"
Show Hana, a young Ethiopian woman in a lavender T-shirt with her curly hair in a high bun, typing privately while two other adult players think over their own phones. Her enlarged phone/callout clearly reads "Festival" and a small annotation OUTSIDE her phone reads "Hana's made-up answer". Include the flamingo question above. Nobody sees the truth yet. Caption "Write a false answer that sounds true."

Scene 2 heading "2  Find the truth"
Focus on Dawit, an Ethiopian man in a muted-lime shirt with short curls, looking thoughtfully at HIS OWN phone. Show an enlarged anonymous ballot on HIS phone with exactly three equally styled choices "Festival", "Flamboyance", "Parade". He is selecting Festival, with a neutral purple selection indicator. Do NOT mark the truth, use any green correct symbol or expose author names in this panel. Hana remains nearby holding her own phone, its screen hidden. Important: it is Dawit's ballot, not Hana's, since nobody can vote for their own answer. Caption "Vote on your phone. Names stay hidden."

Scene 3 heading "3  Reveal & score"
The same people laugh with warm, playful surprise at the shared TV. Hana holds her phone with an amused smile; Dawit points toward the reveal. On the TV, clearly separate two reveal cards:
"Flamboyance" with label "THE TRUTH"
"Festival" with label "Hana's bluff"
Two small person/vote icons beside Festival show that her bluff fooled two people. This panel alone reveals whose bluff it was. Below the scene, include two tidy scoring callouts: "Find the truth: +1,000" and "Fool a player: +500".
Footer in smaller but readable type: "+250 for submitting a bluff. You cannot vote for your own."

Crucial teaching distinction: invent an answer PRIVATELY first; then choose the real answer from ANONYMOUS mixed truth-and-bluff choices; finally the reveal unmasks authors and awards both kinds of points. Never imply the goal is to vote for the funniest answer or one's own bluff. No wagering, money, villain imagery, shame, punishment or elimination. Keep the text concise, accurate, and large enough to read. Friendly clear sans-serif lettering, no additional slogans, labels or explanatory paragraphs. The diagrams are essential but the mood must remain a warm Ethiopian gathering, not a software manual.
